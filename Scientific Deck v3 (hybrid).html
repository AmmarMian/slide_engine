01 · Motivation
04 / 19

Tabular generation resists the tools that work elsewhere.

Mixed continuous and categorical columns, heavy-tailed marginals, and brittle inter-column constraints break GAN- and VAE-based generators.
Mixed types
Continuous, ordinal, and categorical columns coexist; common likelihoods do not.
Multi-modal marginals
Real columns concentrate around discrete modes that smooth densities under-fit.
Privacy & utility
Synthetic tables are deployed when raw data cannot be shared — fidelity matters.

03 · Definition
07 / 19

Discrete diffusion process.

Definition 3.1 — Categorical forward kernel
For a categorical variable x_0 \in \{1,\dots,K\}, the forward process is the Markov chain
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathrm{Cat}\big(x_t;\ \boldsymbol{Q}_t\, \mathbf{e}_{x_{t-1}}\big),
with row-stochastic transition matrix \boldsymbol{Q}_t \in [0,1]^{K\times K} and absorbing state [\mathsf{mask}].
Marginals at any timestep close in form: q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathrm{Cat}(x_t;\ \overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}_t\, \mathbf{e}_{x_0}), where \overline{\boldsymbol{Q}}_t = \boldsymbol{Q}_t \boldsymbol{Q}_{t-1} \cdots \boldsymbol{Q}_1. This makes training tractable: we sample x_t in one step from x_0, no rollout needed.

04 · Objective
08 / 19

Variational bound, simplified.

\mathcal{L}_{\text{vlb}} \;=\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_q\!\left[ -\log p_\theta(x_0 \mid x_1) \right]}_{\text{reconstruction}} \;+\; \sum_{t=2}^{T}\, \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_q\!\left[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, x_0)\,\|\, p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t)\big) \right]}_{\text{denoising terms}}
(1)
We follow the simplified objective of Austin et al. (2021) — a per-timestep cross-entropy on the predicted clean token, weighted by \boldsymbol{w}_t.
\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{t,\,x_0,\,x_t}\!\left[\, \boldsymbol{w}_t \cdot \mathrm{CE}\!\big(\hat x_0(x_t, t),\; x_0\big) \,\right]
(2)

05 · Algorithm
09 / 19

Reverse process, sampling.

Algorithm 1  ·  Ancestral sampling O(T·d)
1:input: trained \hat x_0, schedule \{Q_t\}, length d
2:x_T \leftarrow [\mathsf{mask}]^d
3:for  t = T, T{-}1, \dots, 1  do
4:     \tilde x_0 \sim p_\theta(\,\cdot \mid x_t, t\,)
5:     x_{t-1} \sim q(x_{t-1} \mid x_t,\, \tilde x_0)
6:end for
7:return  x_0
Why it works
Conditioning on the predicted clean sample \tilde x_0 reduces variance: the posterior q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) is closed-form, so each step inherits the model's accuracy.
Compute
For T = 200, d = 64, generation runs in 38 ms / row on a single A100.
